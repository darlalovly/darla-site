  <div class="flex flex-col justify-center space-y-3">
    <p>Talking with a friend about my experience viewing the scenic Mount Rushmore, she asked if I had a chance to see Crazy Horse. At the time, I had no idea such a monument existed.</p>
    <p>When I began my Google search for details on the monument, I discovered <a href="https://www.journeymuseum.org/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" class="underline underline-offset-4 hover:text-neutral-600">The Journey Museum & Learning Center</a> in Rapid City would be hosting a presentation on the carving led by Whitney Rencountre II of the Crow Creek Sioux Tribe and the current CEO of the Crazy Horse Memorial Foundation. The timing was so perfect, I could hardly believe my luck in getting to learn first-hand about this grand feat directly from someone so closely involved.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="mb-8 space-y-4">
  <p>Mr. Rencountre gave an update on the current status and progress of the enormous carving going on at Thunderhead Mountain since 1948. He shared the idea and mission conceived by Chief Henry Standing Bear to creating a monument in the likeness of his cousin and Lakota Warrior, Crazy Horse.</p>
  <p>The purpose and mission of this monument was to honor all the tribes encompassing the North American continent. Standing Bear had a great desire to tell the story of his people and share their cultural traditions and beliefs with everyone around the world. Even though the massive carving is still in progress some fifty-odd years since the first blast, that's exactly what it's doing...telling the story Standing Bear wanted told.</p>
</div>

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 mb-8 items-center">
  <div class="flex flex-col justify-center space-y-3 order-2 md:order-1">
    <p>All throughout this summer, The Journey Museum had a special exhibit entitled Monumental Memories in their Adelson Gallery highlighting important landmarks unique to the Black Hills. It was a great honor to attend the final presentation discussing the progress of the Crazy Horse Monument and the learn about the history behind this colossal endeavor. Once finished, it's expected to be considered the 8th wonder of the world.</p>
