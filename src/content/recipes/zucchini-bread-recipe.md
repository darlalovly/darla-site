recipeExcerpt: |
  A tender, rustic loaf inspired by Paul Prudhomme's classic technique. Featuring toasted pecans, creamy buttermilk, and just enough brown sugar to let the spice shine without being overly sweet.

recipeIngredients: |
  **The Dry Mix:**
  2 cups all-purpose flour
  ¾ cup packed brown sugar
  2 teaspoons baking powder
  ¾ teaspoon ground cinnamon
  ¼ teaspoon salt

  **The Fresh & Nutty:**
  ¾ cup grated zucchini (do not drain or squeeze)
  ½ cup chopped pecans (roasted preferred)

  **The Wet Ingredients:**
  1 stick (½ cup) unsalted butter, melted
  ⅔ cup buttermilk
  1 large egg plus 1 large egg yolk, beaten

recipeInstructions: |
  **Preheat and Prep:** Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Thoroughly grease your 9x5 loaf pan with butter or non-stick spray.
  **Combine Dry Ingredients:** In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, brown sugar, grated zucchini, chopped pecans, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt. Ensure the zucchini is well distributed through the flour.
  **Add the Wet Ingredients:** Pour the beaten eggs, buttermilk, and melted butter into the bowl with the dry ingredients.
  **Mix Gently:** Using a spatula, mix the ingredients until just blended. Be careful not to overmix—a few streaks of flour are perfectly fine. The batter will be thick and rustic.
  **Fill the Pan:** Spoon the batter into your prepared loaf pan, using the spatula to spread the mixture evenly.
  **Bake to Perfection:** Bake for one hour, or until a wooden skewer inserted into the center comes out clean.
  **Cool and Enjoy:** Let the bread cool in the pan for 10–15 minutes before turning it out onto a wire rack to cool completely before slicing.

recipeNotes: |
  **The Moisture Secret:**
  Do not squeeze the water out of your zucchini! That natural moisture is exactly what makes this bread melt-in-your-mouth tender. 
  **The Chef's Legacy:**
  This recipe is lovingly adapted from the Carrot Bread technique in Chef Paul Prudhomme's 'Louisiana Kitchen'. Substituting zucchini and adding buttermilk creates a signature Southern tang that makes this 'Everybody's Favorite.'
  **Roasted Nuts:**
  For the best flavor, toast your pecans in a dry skillet for 3–5 minutes until fragrant before adding them to the dry mix.

<p>There's a special kind of satisfaction that comes from turning a garden gift into something delicious. Today, I'm sharing my favorite, wonderfully moist, perfectly spiced <strong>Zucchini Bread</strong> that strikes the ideal balance of flavor without being too, too sweet.</p>

<p>This recipe is my personal adaptation of Paul Prudhomme's Carrot Muffins or Bread recipe found in his legendary <em>Louisiana Kitchen</em> cookbook. I've been making this recipe since the 90s with a few substitutions over the years. I've always preferred pecans to walnuts, and buttermilk is always my choice over whole milk.</p>

<p>The result is an incredibly tender quick bread. The batter is thick and rustic, promising a loaf that’s substantial yet light, with a beautiful crumb and a crackled, golden top. It’s the kind of recipe you’ll make again and again—it's always been a welcomed gift!</p>
