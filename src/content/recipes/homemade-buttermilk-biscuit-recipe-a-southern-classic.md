recipeExcerpt: |
  The smell of homemade biscuits baking in the oven is pure comfort. Whether you’re making these for a weekend brunch or a holiday gathering, this flaky, buttery Southern classic will quickly become a family favorite.

recipeIngredients: |
  **The Dry Ingredients:**
  4 cups all-purpose flour (Wheat Montana Premium preferred)
  ¼ cup sugar (or no-calorie sweetener)
  2 tablespoons baking powder
  1 teaspoon salt

  **The Fat:**
  2 sticks (1 cup) unsalted butter, frozen
  4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted (for brushing)

  **The Wet Ingredients:**
  1¼ cups buttermilk
  2 large eggs

recipeInstructions: |
  **Preheat & Prep:** Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Generously butter a baking sheet to prevent sticking.
  **Mix Dry:** In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, and salt until well incorporated.
  **Grate the Butter:** Use a box grater to shred the frozen butter directly into the flour mixture. Gently toss until the butter is evenly distributed.
  **Combine Wet:** In a separate bowl, whisk together the buttermilk and eggs until smooth. Slowly add the wet ingredients to the dry mix, stirring gently with a fork until a soft dough forms.
  **Shape:** Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Knead until it comes together and is no longer sticky. Pat the dough to about 2 inches thick. Use a biscuit cutter to cut out your biscuits.
  **Bake:** Place biscuits on the prepared baking sheet with a little space between them. Brush the tops with melted butter. Bake for 14 minutes or until they turn golden brown. Let them cool slightly before serving.

recipeNotes: |
  **Serving Suggestions:**
  Biscuits are best fresh out of the oven! Slather them with creamy melted butter, a drizzle of golden honey, or a spoonful of jam. These also make incredible breakfast sandwiches.
  **The Secret:**
  Grating frozen butter is the absolute secret to those flaky Southern layers. It keeps the fat cold until it hits the oven, creating steam pockets that make the biscuits rise.
  **Maw-Maw's Way:**
  If the dough feels a bit too sticky, don't overwork it with more flour. A light dusting on your hands is all you need to keep that "baby" biscuit texture.
