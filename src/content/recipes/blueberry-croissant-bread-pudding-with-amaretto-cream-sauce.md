recipeIngredients: |
  **For the Bread Pudding:**
  12 full-size bakery croissants (buttery, not sweetened)
  1 pint fresh blueberries
  6 large eggs
  2 cups heavy cream
  1 cup whole milk
  ¾ cup sugar
  1 tsp vanilla extract
  ½ tsp ground cinnamon
  ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
  ¼ tsp salt
  ½ cup sliced almonds (for topping)
  *Butter for greasing the dish*

  **For the Amaretto Cream Sauce:**
  ½ cup heavy cream
  ¼ cup sugar
  1 tbsp unsalted butter
  2–3 tbsp Disaronno amaretto
  Pinch of salt

recipeInstructions: |
  **Preheat & Prep:** Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). Generously butter a 9x13 baking dish.
  **Layer the Dish:** Tear the buttery croissants into large bite-sized pieces and layer them evenly in the dish. Tuck the fresh blueberries in between the layers as you go to ensure every bite has fruit.
  **Make the Custard:** In a large bowl, whisk together the eggs, heavy cream, milk, sugar, vanilla, cinnamon, nutmeg, and salt until completely smooth. 
  **Soak:** Carefully pour the custard over the croissants. Press down gently so the bread can soak up the liquid. Let it sit for 10–15 minutes to fully absorb.
  **Bake:** Sprinkle the sliced almonds over the top. Bake uncovered for 45–55 minutes. You are looking for the top to be beautifully golden brown and the **center to be spongy and set.**
  **The Sauce:** While the pudding bakes, combine the sauce ingredients in a small saucepan over low heat until smooth. Keep warm.
  **Serve:** Serve warm, drizzled generously with the amaretto sauce.

recipeNotes: |
  **The Origin Story:**
  I first made this with leftover croissants from the bakery around the corner. It turned into a Sunday favorite that never really needed tweaking. The richness of the custard, the burst of blueberries, and that almond liqueur finish—it’s hard to beat.
  **Bakery Hack:**
  I usually look for the "day-old" selections at the bakery. Because they are a bit drier, they actually soak up the custard more effectively without losing their structure.
  **Disaronno Substitution:**
  If you want to skip the liqueur, a teaspoon of almond extract in the sauce provides a similar flavor profile.
  **Worth the Mess:**
  Whether you’re planning a special breakfast or need something warm and sweet to end the day, this one’s worth making a mess for.

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Brunch, Reimagined ❤️</h2>

<p>This recipe is part brunch, part dessert, and all comfort. I don’t always remember exact measurements, but this recipe lives somewhere between indulgence and instinct. It’s a little larger than the average bread pudding—I use a full dozen buttery bakery croissants and I never skimp on the blueberries. A whole pint, always fresh.</p>

<p>You can serve this warm out of the oven, but it’s the amaretto cream sauce that really brings it home. That’s Disaronno in the background—and yes, it absolutely goes in the sauce.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">From Simple Stale Bread to Buttery Bliss ✨</h2>

<p>I love bread pudding, but growing up, my mother always just made it with plain old leftover stale white bread. While I do still enjoy that classic version, there is something about using croissants that elevates this dish to a level that's more than just "special."</p>

<p>The laminated layers of a croissant create these incredible pockets that catch the custard and the blueberry juice. They stay buttery and flaky in a way that regular bread just can't mimic. Plus, it’s an incredibly budget-friendly luxury; I can usually find croissants on sale in the day-old bakery selections. I love to finish it off with a handful of sliced almonds for a bit of crunch—it turns a simple breakfast into something truly memorable.</p>

<p>Whether you’re planning a special holiday breakfast or just need something warm and sweet to end a long day, this one's worth making a mess for.</p>
