recipeExcerpt: |
  A rich three-layer chocolate masterpiece featuring a clever 2-ingredient cake base and a unique stability-first Dr. Pepper buttercream. No eggs or oil required—just the magic of carbonation and a splash-proof frosting technique.

recipeIngredients: |
  **For the 2-Ingredient Cake Base:**
  1 box (15.25 oz) Duncan Hines Devil’s Food Cake Mix
  2 mini-cans (approx. 15-16 oz total) Dr. Pepper
  *Butter and flour for greasing pans*

  **For the Dr. Pepper Reduction:**
  1 bottle (16.9 oz) Dr. Pepper (Pure cane sugar preferred)

  **For the Stabilized Cherry-Almond Frosting:**
  1 cup (8 oz) unsalted butter, softened
  1 tbsp almond extract
  1 tsp cherry extract
  1 cup powdered sugar (The "Stabilizer")
  ⅔ cup cooled Dr. Pepper reduction
  3 ½ cups powdered sugar (Added gradually)

  **For the Decoration:**
  8-12 chocolate-covered cherry cordials, halved
  8-12 maraschino cherries, stems removed
  Chocolate sprinkles

recipeInstructions: |
  **Prep the Layers:** Preheat your oven to 350°F. Generously grease and flour three 8-inch round cake pans. Whisk together the cake mix and two mini-cans of Dr. Pepper until perfectly smooth.
  **Bake the Base:** Divide the batter evenly between the three prepared pans. Bake for 20 minutes, or until a toothpick comes out clean. Cool in pans for 10 minutes before inverting onto wire racks to cool completely.
  **Reduce and Refine:** While baking, pour the 16.9 oz Dr. Pepper into a small saucepan. Bring to a boil, then simmer for about 30 minutes until reduced to ⅔ or ¾ cup. Note: It will still be watery and thin, not a thick syrup. Let it cool completely.
  **Whip the Frosting:** Cream the softened butter with almond and cherry extracts until fluffy. Add exactly 1 cup of powdered sugar and mix on low—this creates the "buffer" for the liquid.
  **The Splash-Proof Technique:** With the mixer on its lowest setting, very slowly drizzle in the cooled Dr. Pepper reduction. The sugar buffer prevents the liquid from separating or splashing.
  **Finish the Frosting:** Gradually add the remaining 3 ½ cups of powdered sugar until the frosting is thick, smooth, and spreadable.
  **Assembly:** Frost between the layers and then the top and sides of the cake.
  **Decorate:** Slice your cherry cordials in half. Nestle a maraschino cherry into the center of each half. Space these around the top edge and finish with a heavy dusting of chocolate sprinkles.

recipeNotes: |
  **The "Splash-Proof" Secret:**
  Don't skip that first cup of powdered sugar before adding the liquid. It gives the butter fat something to "grab" onto, which is the only way to get a watery reduction to emulsify into a fluffy buttercream without making a mess of your kitchen.
  **Authentic Flavor:**
  For the most authentic taste, look for Dr. Pepper bottled in Frisco, Texas. They use pure cane sugar instead of corn syrup, which gives the reduction a cleaner, more intense sweetness.
  **Temperature Warning:**
  Never add the Dr. Pepper reduction to the butter while even slightly warm. It must be stone-cold to ensure your frosting stays stable and doesn't "break" or melt the butter.

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">A Mystery Solved with a Splash of Soda ❤️</h2>

<p>The other day, I stumbled across a post on social media about a "Dr. Pepper King Cake." I went down a bit of a rabbit hole trying to find a trace of an actual King Cake made with soda, but while that specific mystery remained unsolved, it led me to a couple of other recipes that I eventually fused into this 3-layer masterpiece.</p>

<p>I was ecstatic at how easy this was to pull off. By splitting the batter between three round 8-inch pans and baking them for just 20 minutes, I achieved a texture that is absolutely fantastic—moist, dark, and rich, without needing a single egg or a drop of oil. It felt like a magic trick.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The Frosting Fiasco (and the Fix) 🥄</h2>

<p>Of course, no baking experiment is without its hurdles. I started by following a frosting recipe written specifically to provide that iconic Dr. Pepper taste. The original instructions suggested the soda would cook down into a thick syrup after about 5 to 7 minutes. After boiling mine for 30 minutes, it was still a very thin liquid—finally reduced to somewhere between 2/3 and 3/4 cup.</p>

<p>Lo and behold if I didn’t end up with that Dr. Pepper reduction splashed all over my kitchen the second I turned on the mixer! After shutting everything down and cleaning up the mess, I realized the butter needed a "buffer." By adding a small amount of powdered sugar to the butter *before* drizzling in the liquid, I created an emulsion that actually held together. My stabilized method below is the only way to go unless you’re looking for an excuse to deep-clean your countertops.</p>
